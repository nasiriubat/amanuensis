# Interview rounds

## Round 1: Scope and questions

- What area does the review cover, and what does it exclude?
- Why is a review needed now? What changed?
- The research questions, in the words you would put in the paper.
- Have there been prior reviews? How does this one differ?

## Round 2: Search

- Which databases or sources were searched? Exact names.
- The query strings used, per database.
- Date range, language and publication type filters.
- Was snowballing (backward or forward) done? From which seed papers?

## Round 3: Screening and extraction

- Inclusion and exclusion criteria as written before screening.
- Counts at each stage: retrieved, after duplicates, after title and abstract, after
  full text, final set.
- How many reviewers screened? How were disagreements resolved?
- What was extracted from each paper? Was there a form or a spreadsheet?
- Was study quality assessed? With which instrument?

## Round 4: Findings

- The classification scheme or taxonomy, and how it was derived.
- Per research question, the main finding in one sentence with a count.
- Contradictions or disagreements across papers.
- Trends over time worth showing.

## Round 5: Gaps and validity

- Which cells of the taxonomy are empty or thin? Why does that matter?
- Concrete recommendations for researchers and for practitioners.
- Threats to validity of the review: what might have been missed and why.
- Where is the extraction data available?
