# Literature review

A systematic or semi-systematic review that maps a research area, synthesises what is
known and identifies gaps. The contribution is the synthesis and the classification,
not the list of papers.

## What reviewers expect

- Explicit research questions the review answers.
- A documented and repeatable search: databases, query strings, dates, inclusion and
  exclusion criteria, number of papers at each screening stage.
- A classification scheme or taxonomy that organises the findings.
- Synthesis: patterns, contradictions, trends, and gaps, with counts where they help.
- A clear statement of what practitioners and researchers should take away.
- Threats to validity of the review itself: search coverage, selection bias, single
  reviewer extraction.

## Common reasons for rejection

- Search process not reproducible or not described at all.
- The review is a list of summaries with no synthesis across papers.
- No research questions, or questions that the results never return to.
- Inclusion criteria that appear tailored to a preferred conclusion.
- Gaps identified are generic ("more empirical work is needed").
- Missing well-known work that a reviewer immediately notices.

## Framing moves that work

- Explain why a review is needed now: growth of the area, fragmentation, a recent
  shift.
- Show the screening funnel as a figure with counts at each stage.
- Present the taxonomy early and use it to structure the results.
- Make the gap analysis concrete: which combinations in the taxonomy are empty, and
  why that matters.
