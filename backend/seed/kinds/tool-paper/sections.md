# Default sections

Typical lengths assume an 8 to 12 page conference paper. Adjust to the venue page limit.
A learned playbook from exemplars overrides this order when they disagree.

1. **Abstract** (150 to 250 words). Problem, tool, what is new, evidence, one result.
2. **Introduction** (1 to 1.5 pages). The pain with a scenario; why existing tools fall
   short; the tool in one paragraph; contributions as a list; paper structure in two
   sentences at most.
3. **Background and motivation** (0.5 to 1 page). Domain concepts the reader needs;
   the motivating example that returns later. Merge into the introduction if short.
4. **Related work** (0.75 to 1.5 pages). Grouped by approach, not listed by paper.
   Ends with a positioning paragraph: what this tool does that none of them do.
5. **Design** or **Approach** (1.5 to 3 pages). Requirements or design goals;
   architecture with a figure; each major component with the decision behind it and
   the alternative rejected; how the running example flows through.
6. **Implementation** (0.5 to 1 page). Stack, size, notable engineering, availability.
   Often merged into Design.
7. **Evaluation** (1.5 to 3 pages). Research questions; method; participants or
   dataset; results per question with tables or figures; threats to validity.
8. **Discussion** (0.5 to 1 page). What the results mean, where the tool fits, what
   it cannot do, lessons for others building similar tools.
9. **Conclusion and future work** (0.25 to 0.5 page). Restate the contribution in
   new words; two or three specific next steps.
10. **Data and tool availability** (a few lines, if the venue expects it).
