# Position paper

A paper that argues for a view, a research direction or a change in practice. The
contribution is the argument: a claim, the reasons for it, the evidence that supports
it and an honest treatment of the objections.

## What reviewers expect

- A claim stated in one sentence that a reasonable colleague could disagree with.
- Reasons organised, not a stream of opinions.
- Evidence drawn from literature, experience or preliminary data, cited properly.
- The strongest counterarguments presented fairly and answered.
- Concrete implications: what should researchers or practitioners do differently?
- A research agenda or call to action with specific items.

## Common reasons for rejection

- The claim is something nobody disputes.
- Opinion without evidence, or evidence that is anecdote presented as fact.
- Counterarguments ignored or strawmanned.
- Vague call to action: "the community should pay more attention to".
- Reads as a literature review or a proposal in disguise.

## Framing moves that work

- Open with a tension or a failure everyone has seen.
- State the claim early, in italics or as its own paragraph.
- Number the reasons and give each its own subsection.
- Give the objections a section of their own with a sub-heading per objection.
- End with a numbered agenda with something someone could start tomorrow.
