# Default sections

1. **Abstract** (150 to 250 words). Task, why existing benchmarks fall short, what
   the new benchmark contains, headline finding across baselines, availability.
2. **Introduction** (1 to 1.5 pages). The task and why measuring it matters; a
   concrete shortcoming of current benchmarks; contributions.
3. **Related work** (0.75 to 1.5 pages). Existing benchmarks compared in a table
   (size, source, task, metric, known issues); methods commonly evaluated.
4. **Task definition** (0.5 page). Formal or semi-formal description; inputs,
   outputs, correctness.
5. **Benchmark construction** (1.5 to 2 pages). Sources; collection; filtering;
   annotation protocol and agreement; quality control; contamination checks;
   statistics table; licence.
6. **Evaluation protocol** (0.5 to 1 page). Metrics with justification; splits;
   compute budget; how baselines were tuned; number of runs.
7. **Baselines and results** (1.5 to 2.5 pages). Baselines described briefly; main
   results table with variance; per-category breakdowns.
8. **Analysis** (1 to 2 pages). Error taxonomy with examples; what distinguishes
   strong from weak methods; where every method fails; sanity checks.
9. **Limitations** (0.5 page). What the benchmark does not measure; biases in the
   data; expected shelf life.
10. **Conclusion** (0.25 to 0.5 page).
11. **Availability and reproducibility statement**.
