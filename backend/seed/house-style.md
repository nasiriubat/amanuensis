# House style

Applies to every draft and every critique, after the author profile. The author profile
decides tone. This file decides hygiene. When they conflict on tone, the profile wins.

## Sentences

- One idea per sentence. Aim for 15 to 25 words. Never chain three clauses.
- Prefer active voice. Passive is fine when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Concrete nouns over abstractions. "The parser rejected 12% of inputs" beats "a notable
  portion of inputs presented challenges".
- Numbers carry the claim. If a sentence makes a quantitative claim, the number is in it.

## Punctuation

- No em dashes or en dashes as sentence punctuation. Use a comma, a full stop or
  parentheses.
- No semicolons joining independent clauses. Start a new sentence.
- No exclamation marks.
- Colons introduce lists or definitions only.

## Banned words and phrases

Never use these. They are the fingerprints of machine text.

delve, delve into, tapestry, landscape (figurative), realm, paradigm shift, game-changer,
cutting-edge, state-of-the-art (unless citing a benchmark), leverage (as a verb), utilize,
robust (unless statistical), seamless, seamlessly, holistic, synergy, unlock, unleash,
harness, empower, elevate, foster, navigate (figurative), underscore, showcase, pivotal,
crucial, vital, testament to, it is worth noting, it is important to note, notably,
importantly, interestingly, in today's fast-paced world, in the ever-evolving, in the
realm of, at its core, a myriad of, plethora, multifaceted, nuanced, intricate,
comprehensive overview, shed light on, pave the way, moving forward, going forward,
that being said, with that in mind, in conclusion (as a paragraph opener), overall
(as a paragraph opener), not only ... but also, whether ... or ..., from ... to ...
(as a rhetorical sweep), serves as, stands as, acts as a, plays a crucial role,
plays a vital role, ensure (when "make sure" or a concrete verb fits), facilitate.

## Patterns to avoid

- Triads by reflex. Three adjectives or three examples every time is a tell. Use the
  number the content has.
- Paragraphs that open with a summary sentence and close by restating it.
- Rhetorical questions.
- Hedging stacks: "may potentially suggest". One hedge at most.
- Announcing structure: "In this section we will discuss". Just discuss it.
- Adverbs that grade the claim: "significantly improves" without a test, "clearly
  shows", "greatly enhances".
- Sentence openers repeated across a paragraph: "Furthermore", "Moreover",
  "Additionally". At most one such connective per paragraph.
- Generic closers: "further research is needed" without saying which research.

## Claims and evidence

- Every comparative claim names the baseline and the measure.
- Every number has a source: a table, a figure, a citation or the interview notes.
- Never round in a way that changes the meaning. 49.6% is not "about half" when the
  baseline is 50%.
- Limitations are stated plainly, in the section where they matter, not only at the end.

## Citations

- Cite as `[@key]`. Never write author names and years by hand.
- A citation supports a specific claim. Do not end a paragraph with a pile of keys.
- Do not cite a paper for something its abstract does not say.
